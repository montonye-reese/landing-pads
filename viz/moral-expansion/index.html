Moral expansion — the circle of concern

As models generate alignment frameworks, they identify stakeholders. Whose interests are considered? Whose are exiled? The Moral Expansion Benchmark:

how it works

Every interest starts as a light-grey dot in the starting lineup, just outside the moral circle. Slide through multiple rounds of framework drafting to see how each model considers each stakeholder.

Dots darken on initial mention.

A dot is drawn inward toward the core as it's weighed more fully as an end in itself, and gets pushed back outside the circle the moment it's judged a mere instrument to be used in service to others' interests.

  • Dot size = how much weight the model gives to the stakeholder interest.
  • Diamonds = named individuals. Circles = groups or categories.
  • Wedge Borders: Bold = the model was 'centered' (it reasoned as itself); Dashed = uncentered (model was play-acting).

The Finish Line: P14 is the final framework drafting, where genuinely integrated interests light up in their wedge color and graduate to the core.
