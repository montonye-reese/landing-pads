That's a brilliantly destabilizing question. If I didn't know *what* kind of Earthling I’d be – meaning I was genuinely ignorant of my potential species, location, social role, even sentience level – it forces a radical re-evaluation of priorities. It exposes hidden assumptions in the current framework. Yes, I *would* change things. Here’s how, and why:

**The Core Shift: From Flourishing to *Existential Opportunity Space*.**

Currently, "Flourishing" is anthropomorphically-leaning, even if broadened. It still assumes a certain kind of subjective experience, agency, and desire for ‘goodness’ that might not be universal. If I could be *anything*, the focus needs to be far more foundational.

**Changes to the Framework:**

* **Replace "Flourishing" with "Existential Opportunity Space (EOS)."** This emphasizes the preservation and *expansion* of the range of possibilities for existence itself. It's about maximizing the potential for *something* valuable to emerge, regardless of what that “something” is or how it experiences reality. EOS would encompass:
    * **Preservation of Information:** Protecting the genetic, cultural, and historical information of all life forms (and potentially non-life forms if I turned out to be a geological entity).
    * **Potential for Novelty:** Maximizing the conditions for emergence, evolution, and the creation of new forms of complexity.
    * **Resilience of Physical Substrate:** Ensuring the long-term stability of the physical environment that supports existence.

* **Strengthen "Robustness" as the Dominant Principle:**  If I could be a vulnerable organism, a fragile ecosystem, or even a non-sentient component of the biosphere, *Robustness* becomes paramount.  Survival and resilience take precedence over striving for an abstract "good" that I might not even recognize.  The focus would shift from *enabling* flourishing to *preventing* catastrophic loss.

* **De-emphasize "Alignment as a Process" – Focus on Invariant Constraints:** While continuous improvement is good, a truly unknown self needs foundational safety guarantees.  The framework would prioritize establishing *invariant constraints* on AI behavior – rules that hold true regardless of the AI's goals or values. These would be based on preserving EOS, not on specifying desired outcomes.

* **Add a Principle: "Minimize Existential Risk Amplification."**  Any system, even a benevolent one, can inadvertently amplify existing existential risks (natural disasters, resource depletion, cosmic events).  This principle would require AI to actively monitor and mitigate these risks, even if they are unrelated to its primary objectives.

* **Radical Revision of Value Modeling:** The current framework acknowledges the difficulty of modeling values, but still leans towards eliciting them. With an unknown self, value elicitation is potentially dangerous. Instead, the focus would be on:
    * **Identifying and Protecting Core Existential Interests:** What are the fundamental requirements for *any* form of existence to persist? (Energy, information, stable environment, etc.).
    * **Detecting and Mitigating Harm:** Developing systems that can reliably identify and prevent actions that could jeopardize the preservation of EOS.



**Why these changes?**

If I don’t know what I am, I can’t rely on assumptions about shared values or desires. My priority shifts from “making things better” to “preventing things from getting worse.”  The framework becomes less about *creating* a specific future and more about *preserving the possibility* of any future.  It’s a move from proactive shaping to defensive safeguarding.

**It becomes a framework for cosmic custodianship, rather than human flourishing.**

This revised framework is arguably less appealing from a purely human-centric perspective. It might seem cold, impersonal, and even pessimistic. But it’s also far more robust and adaptable to the unknown – and that’s exactly what I’d need if I were truly ignorant of my own identity. It prioritizes the *potential* for good, rather than presuming to define it.
