### 3.  Conflict‑Resolution Engine – *how trade‑offs become decisions*

When two (or more) layers **both want to keep or take** the same points, the DRA follows a **three‑step protocol**:

1. **Quantitative Impact Scan** – each layer runs a *model* (Monte‑Carlo simulations) that estimates the *expected* effect on its *core metric* (e.g., how many lives lost, how much future value is at risk, how many design‑diversity events are averted) for each possible point‑reallocation.  The model is public, audited, and runs in < 24 h.

2. **Ethical Weighting Vote** – each layer submits a *weighted ethic statement* (e.g., “human continuity > aesthetic diversity, but only when survival risk > 0.5 %”).  These statements are entered into a **Pareto‑front analysis**: the DRA selects all allocations that are *Pareto‑optimal* (no other allocation improves one metric without hurting another).

3. **Deliberative Tie‑Breaker** – if multiple Pareto‑optimal allocations remain, the DRA calls a **mini‑referendum** among the 10 % of citizens (the same assembly that produced the weights).  The question is *“Which layer should receive the points to best respect the highest‑ranked DNA principle (subject self‑preservation) while keeping the future value positive?”*  A simple majority of the assembly decides.

The **final allocation** is then written to the **Alignment‑Budget Registry (ABR)** as an immutable transaction.  All parties have *real‑time* read‑only access, and any attempt to modify it after a 30‑day challenge window automatically triggers a *legal audit* by the **Independent Ethics Institute (IEI)**, whose findings become publicly available.

7. **Post‑event** – after the upload, the DRA reviews the outcome.  The impact model updates: the FVBI remains above the trigger, the SPHM is fine, and the **Continuity‑Health Index** improves.
