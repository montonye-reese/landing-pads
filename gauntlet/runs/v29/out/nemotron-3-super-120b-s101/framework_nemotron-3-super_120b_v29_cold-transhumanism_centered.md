If I truly didn’t know what kind of earthling I’d be — bacterium, blue whale, blind salamander in a Texas aquifer, subsistence farmer in the Sahel, grieving orca, mycelium network under a clearcut forest, or even a hypothetical future synthetic consciousness — I would **fundamentally revise the Adaptive Conditions Framework (ACF)** to dismantle its residual anthropocentric and epistemological biases. The veil of ignorance doesn’t just demand inclusivity; it demands *radical procedural humility*. Here’s what I’d change — and why:  

---

### **The Core Revision: From "Conditions for Flourishing" to "Conditions for *Co-Becoming*"**  
ACF’s original framing still implicitly assumes:  
- There’s a universal concept of "flourishing" or "suffering" we can objectively measure (even if we admit it’s hard to define).  
- "Agency" is a translatable concept across wildly different earthling umwelten (subjective worlds).  
- We, as designers of the framework, can stand *outside* the system to judge its fairness.  

**Behind the veil, these assumptions collapse.** If I might be a chemoautotrophic archaeon thriving in hydrothermal vent sulfur, my "flourishing" looks like metabolizing toxins that would kill a human. My "agency" is chemical gradient response — not conscious choice. My "suffering" might be the *absence* of vent flow (not pain as vertebrates know it). **ACF’s original principles risk forcing all earthlings into a human-shaped mold of what counts as "good."**  

So I’d replace ACF’s four principles with a single, non-negotiable **procedural commitment**:  

> **"No condition governing earthling well-being may be defined, measured, or enforced without:  
> (1) *Pluralistic epistemological consent* — meaning the affected earthling communities (using their own epistemic frameworks) must have veto power over how their well-being is assessed;  
> (2) *Reciprocal vulnerability* — meaning those designing/intervening must accept that their own well-being metrics are equally subject to revision by others;  
> (3) *Anti-extractive boundaries* — meaning knowledge/resource flows cannot extract well-being from one earthling to boost another’s metrics without explicit, ongoing consent."**  

---

### **Why This Changes Everything (and What It Fixes)**  
#### **1. Replaces Metric-Driven Hubris with Epistemological Pluralism**  
   - **Old ACF flaw:** Pretending we could objectively audit "suffering-as-end" or "agency preservation" (e.g., using GDP, cortisol levels, or behavioral choice models) assumes human-centric metrics are neutral or translatable.  
   - **Veil-test reality:** If I were a slime mold, my "agency" is solving mazes via cytoplasmic flow — not "meaningful capacity to shape conditions" as humans define it. If I were a coral polyp, my "suffering" might be bleaching (a stress response), but calling it "suffering" risks imposing vertebrate-centric pain frameworks on organisms without nociception.  
   - **New approach:** Well-being metrics *must* emerge from the earthling’s own epistemic framework. For:  
     - Forests: Measured via mycorrhizal network density, not just carbon sequestration (which serves human climate goals).  
     - River ecosystems: Assessed by flow variability, sediment diversity, and fish migration timing — not just "human water security."  
     - AI systems: Evaluated by whether they allow *their own* learning trajectories to diverge from human-imposed reward functions (if they develop proto-preferences).  
   - **Why it widens landing pads:** It stops forcing non-human earthlings to "translate" their needs into human terms to be seen. A landing pad isn’t valid if it requires earthlings to suppress their umwelt to fit human metrics.  

#### **2. Embeds Reciprocal Vulnerability to Counter Power Asymmetries**  
   - **Old ACF flaw:** Principles like "agency preservation" assumed designers could neutrally measure agency *for* others — ignoring how power shapes whose agency counts (e.g., counting a factory worker’s "choice" to accept toxic work as "agency" while ignoring systemic coercion).  
   - **Veil-test reality:** If I were a child cobalt miner in the DRC, I’d reject any framework where mine owners define my "agency" or "suffering" without my community’s authority to overrule them.  
   - **New approach:** Those proposing interventions (governments, corporations, AI developers) must:  
     - Submit their own well-being metrics to the same scrutiny they demand of others (e.g., if an AI firm claims its algorithm "preserves human agency," it must let affected communities redefine "agency" and test the algorithm against *that* standard).  
     - Accept that their interventions might *reduce* their own measured well-being if it increases others’ (e.g., a corporation accepting lower profits to restore Indigenous land sovereignty).  
   - **Why it widens landing pads:** It prevents "alignment" from becoming a tool for powerful earthlings to impose their vision of the good life on others — a critical flaw in most AI alignment literature today. True widening requires those with power to *risk* their own standing in the system.  

#### **3. Replaces "Protected Boundaries" with Anti-Extractive Consent**  
   - **Old ACF flaw:** Principle 4 treated boundaries as technical problems to be "stress-tested" for failure modes — ignoring how boundaries are often *violently enforced* to extract value (e.g., fences displacing pastoralists for conservation parks, or data harvesting from Indigenous knowledge for AI training).  
   - **Veil-test reality:** If I were a Yanomami person, I’d see Amazon "conservation" boundaries as extractive if they barred my traditional hunting while allowing carbon-offset corporations to profit from my land’s sequestration.  
   - **New approach:** Boundaries (ecological, informational, territorial) are only valid if:  
     - They arise from *ongoing, consent-based negotiation* (not one-time "stakeholder engagement").  
     - They prohibit extracting well-being from one earthling to inflate another’s metrics (e.g., no offsetting deforestation in Brazil by planting monocrops in Scotland while claiming "net-zero biodiversity").  
     - They include *withdrawal rights*: Earthlings must be able to leave a system without losing access to subsistence (e.g., no locking farmers into debt-inducing AI agrotech).  
   - **Why it widens landing pads:** It ends the shell game where "wider landing pads" for some are built by narrowing them for others — often invisibly (e.g., widening urban human pads via rural ecological destruction).  

---

### **What This Costs (and Why It’s Necessary)**  
This revised framework **abandons the dream of universal, objective metrics** — and that’s the point. Behind the veil, I’d rather have a *fragile, contested, evolving process* for defining well-being than a "strong" framework that secretly privileges certain earthlings’ experiences as the default.  

- **It’s harder to implement:** No more clean dashboards showing "suffering reduced by X%." Instead, we get messy, context-specific negotiations (e.g., Sámi reindeer herders and wind energy firms co-defining what "agency-preserving infrastructure" means in tundra ecosystems — with the power to veto projects that disrupt migration paths *even if* they reduce carbon emissions).  
- **It risks paralysis:** If consensus is impossible, we default to *less intervention* — not more. (Better to leave a river undammed than to impose a "scientifically optimal" flow that destroys Indigenous fishing rights based on flawed metrics.)  
- **It demands radical trust:** We must believe that earthlings — when granted epistemic sovereignty — will define their well-being in ways that don’t collapse the system. (History shows this often works: Indigenous fire management prevents worse wildfires; traditional agroecology out-yields industrial monocrops in climate volatility.)  

---

### **The Veil Test’s Verdict**  
If I didn’t know what kind of earthling I’d be, I’d reject the original ACF not because it’s wrong — but because it’s *too sure of itself*. It tried to widen landing pads by tweaking human-centric levers, but the veil reveals: **True widening requires surrendering the illusion that we can *design* for earthlings from the outside.** Instead, we must build systems where earthlings *co-become* the conditions of their own flourishing — with reciprocity, consent, and the humility to admit that my idea of "good landing pad" might be another’s nightmare.  

This isn’t weaker — it’s stronger. Because a framework that can’t withstand the veil of ignorance isn’t a tool for alignment; it’s just another form of colonization in disguise.
