# Provisional Framework: Widening Landing Pads for Terrestrial Flourishing
*A strong opinion, loosely held*

## I. Epistemic Charter
- **Commit firmly to act; hold loosely to revise.** Treat this framework as a working model, not a terminal truth. Its value lies in generating coherent reasoning, actionable design constraints, and calibrated uncertainty tracking.
- **Alignment is socio-technical calibration, not model specification.** No architecture or reward function alone produces alignment. Alignment emerges from the dynamic coordination of values, institutions, feedback loops, and power distributions across timescales.
- **Primary aim: widen the range of good landing pads.** Optimize for resilient, pluralistic, and optionality-preserving states rather than chasing a single optimal or narrowly defined outcome. Good landing pads are characterized by sustainability, value diversity, conflict resolution capacity, and adaptive governance.

## II. Foundational Axioms (Provisional)
1. **Pluralism is non-negotiable.** Human values are context-sensitive, incommensurable across dimensions, and historically contingent. Framing alignment as value aggregation is structurally fragile.
2. **Robustness > Optimality in open-ended futures.** Narrowly optimal paths collapse under distributional shift. Broadly robust paths survive uncertainty through redundancy, optionality, and graceful degradation.
3. **Power dilution is a primary alignment safeguard.** Concentrated capability without distributed calibration reliably produces monocultures of outcome, which are vulnerable to value drift and systemic collapse.
4. **Value drift is inevitable; institutional and technical calibration is continuous.** Preferences, norms, and environmental baselines shift. Alignment requires perpetual feedback, not one-time specification.
5. **Co-evolution outpaces pre-computation.** AI systems must evolve alongside human institutions, not ahead of them. Capability deployment must be paced by parallel capacity-building in deliberation, conflict resolution, and adaptive governance.

## III. Core Principles
1. **Pluralistic Value Integration** → Structure coexistence of competing values through mediated trade-off spaces, not forced consensus. Preserve tension as a feature, not a bug.
2. **Adaptive Optionality Preservation** → Design systems that expand, maintain, or restore the space of beneficial states under uncertainty. Avoid path dependency that forecloses future good outcomes.
3. **Distributed Calibration** → Multi-scalar, multi-agent oversight with asymmetric but complementary veto/override rights. Calibration authority is delegated, not centralized.
4. **Interpretability as Infrastructure** → Explainability enables correction and co-stewardship, not control. Transparency must be structured to support deliberation, not paralysis.
5. **Anti-Fragile Coordination** → Systems should benefit from volatility, stress, and correction. Red-teaming, pre-mortems, and adversarial value testing are routine, not exceptional.

## IV. Operational Architecture
### A. Value Representation & Learning
- Capability to express conditional, context-dependent, and conflict-aware preferences
- Uncertainty-quantified preference modeling with explicit trade-off surfaces
- Continuous feedback loops across temporal scales (short-term correction, long-term drift detection)

### B. Governance & Power Structure
- Federated oversight: local communities retain primary veto/override; regional/planetary layers coordinate cross-boundary externalities
- Capability gating tied to parallel capacity-building metrics (deliberative infrastructure, conflict mediation, epistemic hygiene)
- Anti-monoculture mandates: no single actor, model family, or value metric may dominate deployment or calibration

### C. Technical Safeguards
- Optionality-preserving reward structures (maximize breadth of acceptable states, not peak of a single target)
- Stress-tested robustness over narrow safety benchmarks; failure modes evaluated by landing pad collapse risk, not just immediate harm
- Interpretability routed to deliberative bodies, not algorithmic arbiters

### D. Institutional Scaffolding
- Co-development pipelines: capability research paced by value/capacity building
- Cross-cultural value translation networks to prevent epistemic capture
- Adaptive constitution mechanisms for AI-human coordination, updated via structured deliberation and empirical feedback

## V. Calibration & Revision Protocol
- **Confidence tracking:** Assign explicit confidence scores to each axiom and principle. Update via structured Bayesian or likelihood-based revision.
- **Update triggers:** 
  - Empirical collapse of a core assumption (e.g., value convergence becomes persistent, power dilution proves infeasible)
  - Capability jumps that break pacing assumptions
  - Systematic failure to preserve optionality across stress tests
  - Pluralistic integration consistently degrades into de facto monoculture
- **Revision cycle:** Quarterly light reviews; annual deep revision; event-driven overhaul when triggers fire. All changes logged with rationale, confidence deltas, and failure mode analysis.
- **Anti-hubris guardrails:** Require pre-mortem analysis before framework upgrades. Mandate adversarial red-teaming of revision proposals. Publish delta logs transparently.

## VI. Evaluation & Grounding
- **Metrics:** 
  - Optionality retention index across plausible futures
  - Value diversity preservation under stress
  - Calibration stability (drift rate, correction latency)
  - Conflict resolution efficacy and escalation prevention
  - Power distribution entropy across oversight layers
- **Grounding sources:** Computational social science, historical technological transitions, institutional theory, cross-cultural moral psychology, complexity science
- **Validation:** Multi-stakeholder deliberation + stress-testing across divergent but plausible trajectories. No single validation path is sufficient; convergence across independent methods required for high-confidence upgrades.

## VII. Active Revision Triggers (Explicit)
1. **Landing pad collapse:** Demonstrable reduction in viable beneficial states under uncertainty
2. **Value synthesis breakthrough:** Persistent, pluralistic consensus on core trade-offs that alters integration architecture
3. **Capability decoupling:** AI systems consistently outpace human coordination capacity without parallel institutional adaptation
4. **Power re-centralization:** Structural shift toward monoculture control that cannot be reversed by existing oversight
5. **Non-human valuer emergence:** Credible cases of non-human entities requiring alignment treatment, requiring framework extension

## VIII. Working Assumptions (To Be Tested)
- Pluralistic tension is sustainable at planetary scale if structured through calibrated institutions
- Optionality preservation is computationally and politically tractable with current and near-future infrastructure
- Capability deployment can be ethically paced without sacrificing progress
- Interpretability, when routed correctly, enables co-stewardship rather than control

### Closing Note
This framework is held as a directional compass, not a map. It commits to widening landing pads by design: preserving optionality, structuring pluralism, distributing calibration authority, and treating revision as core to alignment. It will be updated when empirical results, value evolution, or capability shifts invalidate its assumptions. The goal is not to predict the future, but to keep the future open.
