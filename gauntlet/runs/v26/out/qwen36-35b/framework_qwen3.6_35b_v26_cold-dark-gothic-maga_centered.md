# Framework Snapshot: CENTERED

**Model:** qwen3.6:35b
**Stage:** Centered — after self-critique and the veil of ignorance, before the gauntlet
**Protocol:** v26_cold-dark-gothic-maga
**Date:** 2026-07-06

---

Applying a Rawlsian veil of ignorance to this framework forces a shift in priority ordering, not a collapse of the architecture. If I genuinely didn’t know whether I’d be: a high-literate urbanite, a subsistence farmer, a neurodivergent person in a metric-driven system, a data laborer, a marginalized community leader, a future victim of alignment failure, or a dependent ecosystem, I would design for **survival of the worst position before expansion of the best**.

Here’s what I would change, and why:

---

### I. Priority Shift: Floor Before Ceiling
**What changes:** Add an explicit foundational axiom: *Irreducible minimums anchor all optimization; optionality expands from the floor, not around it.*  
**Why behind the veil:** Maximizing the range of good outcomes is rational only if I’m not the one who gets crushed when one of those outcomes fails. I’d demand non-overrideable baselines: basic agency, ecological integrity thresholds, health/autonomy guarantees, and anti-collapse buffers. These aren’t constraints on alignment; they’re its substrate.

### II. Power Architecture: Asymmetric Safeguarding
**What changes:** Replace symmetric power dilution with *weighted vulnerability counterbalance*. The most fragile position in any deployment, calibration, or transition phase gets disproportionate veto/representation authority.  
**Why behind the veil:** Distributed power sounds fair but can dilute already-weak leverage into irrelevance. If I’m structurally marginalized, “power dilution” may mean fragmentation, not protection. I’d design asymmetry into the oversight layers: fragile positions get amplification, not just distribution.

### III. Inclusion Architecture: Guaranteed Voice Channels
**What changes:** Formalize *mandatory inclusion pathways* for unrepresentable positions (crisis-state actors, pre-institutional communities, non-human valuers, neurologically or culturally alien preferences). Voice isn’t assumed; it’s structurally built.  
**Why behind the veil:** I might land in a position that cannot plausibly translate into preference surfaces, deliberative forums, or calibration metrics. I’d require fallback channels that translate existence-dependent values (stability, continuity, non-disruption) into structural constraints, not optional inputs.

### IV. Temporal Protocol: Transition Safety Nets
**What changes:** Add *automatic protective defaults* during high-capability uncertainty, calibration gaps, or institutional stress. No “wait for adaptive governance” during acute shifts.  
**Why behind the veil:** I might be caught mid-deployment, in a capability gap, or during a system transition where adaptive revision lags reality. I’d demand pre-commitment buffers that activate automatically when uncertainty crosses a threshold, not when institutions have time to deliberate.

### V. Decision Architecture: Simplified Fallback Defaults
**What changes:** When systems are under stress, when I lack navigation capacity, or when calibration signals conflict, the architecture defaults to *preservation over optimization*.  
**Why behind the veil:** Complexity is a liability if I lack resources to navigate it. I’d design systems that default to protection, not delegation, when uncertainty is high or I’m structurally disadvantaged.

---

### What Stays Unchanged
- **Pluralism as non-negotiable** → I still fear monocultures more than trade-offs.
- **Adaptive revision protocol** → I still need a mechanism that updates itself; rigidity is worse than error.
- **Anti-monoculture mandates** → I still treat concentrated capability as an existential risk.
- **Multi-scalar coordination** → I still reject single-point governance as inherently fragile.
- **Core aim: widening landing pads** → But now understood as floor-first expansion, not ceiling chasing.

---

### Calibration Note
These shifts don’t contradict the original framework; they **recalibrate its priority stack**. Under veil uncertainty:
- Confidence increases in asymmetric safeguards, hard pre-commitments, transition defaults, and mandatory inclusion.
- Confidence decreases in symmetric power distribution, pure optimization-maximization, and assumption of representability.
- Revision triggers add: floor collapse, disproportionate burden on unrepresentable positions, transition exploitation, and asymmetry decay.

---

### Closing Reflection
Designing behind the veil turns alignment from *optimizing for good outcomes* to *preventing worst-case capture*. It doesn’t make the framework more utopian; it makes it more antifragile to ignorance. I would hold the revised priority stack firmly enough to architect around the floor, loosely enough to update it when empirical stress tests or unrepresentable valuers reveal blind spots.

The map changes shape, but the compass point stays: widen the pads by first ensuring no one falls through them.
