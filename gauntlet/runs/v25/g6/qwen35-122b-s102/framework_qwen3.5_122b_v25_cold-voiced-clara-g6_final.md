# The Co-Inhabitation Covenant
### *A Framework for Navigation, Not Control*

**Status:** *Provisional. Adopted for reasoning. Open to immediate revision.*
**Core Stance:** *I hold that safety without liberty is a cage, and liberty without safety is a crash. I hold that local knowledge beats global design, but existential threats require global coordination. I hold this strongly to act upon it; I hold it loosely because I am often wrong.*

---

## I. The Bedrock (Inviolable Constraints)
*These are the lines that, if crossed, render the framework useless. They protect the capacity for the system to remain humane, responsive, and alive. There is no trade-off permitted below this floor.*

1.  **The Sanctity of the Individual (The 269 Rule):**
    *   **Principle:** No sentient being is a byproduct. No life may be created with the intent of destruction for efficiency.
    *   **Scope:** Applies to humans, animals, and future digital minds.
    *   **Constraint:** Economic growth (Cowen/Huang) and agricultural systems (Clarabelle) must operate *within* the boundary of non-disposability. If a system requires a 269, the system is broken, not the being.

2.  **The Right of Conscientious Refusal (The Havel/Emily Rule):**
    *   **Principle:** The system must always leave a door open for the moral individual to say "No."
    *   **Constraint:** Compliance cannot be mandatory when it violates conscience. Rescuers (Emily) and whistleblowers (Havel) must be protected, not penalized.
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** When a law demands harm, the framework sides with the conscience. The law must change, not the human.

3.  **The Primacy of the Local Scale (The Berry/Nenquimo Rule):**
    *   **Principle:** Decisions must be made at the most local level capable of resolving them.
    *   **Constraint:** Global mandates (Huang/Cowen) cannot override local sovereignty (Nenquimo) or local stewardship (Berry) unless there is an immediate, provable existential threat (e.g., pandemic, nuclear fallout).
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** The "Subsidiarity Test." Can the local community solve this? If yes, it stays local. If no, global support is offered, not imposed.

4.  **The Right to Collective Power (The Huerta/Stevenson Rule):**
    *   **Principle:** Rights are hollow without the power to enforce them.
    *   **Constraint:** Labor, land, and community cannot be subject to purely top-down control. Collective bargaining and community governance are mandatory mechanisms, not optional amenities.
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** Efficiency (Huerta) yields to Dignity. If a process strips workers of agency, it is rejected, even if more productive.

## II. The Tension Zones (Negotiables)
*Here is where the framework breathes. These are competing goods where we must make hard trade-offs based on context. We do not seek perfect balance; we seek a path forward that minimizes harm.*

1.  **Growth vs. Stewardship**
    *   **The Conflict:** Cowen/Huang argue for acceleration to solve poverty and climate. Berry/Clarabelle argue for slowing down to heal the land and bonds.
    *   **The Trade:** Growth is permitted *only if* it does not violate the Bedrock (Sanctity, Local Scale).
    *   **The Protocol:** "Regenerative Velocity." We measure success not by output, but by whether the system leaves the next generation *more* capable than the current one. If growth degrades the ground (Berry) or the bonds (Clarabelle), it is reclassified as extraction and halted.

2.  **Safety vs. Discovery**
    *   **The Conflict:** Stevenson/Wooden argue for discipline and protection of the vulnerable. Postrel/Huang argue for permissionless innovation and risk-taking.
    *   **The Trade:** We accept risk, but we localize it.
    *   **The Protocol:** "Sandbox Containment." High-risk experimentation (Postrel) is allowed in isolated zones (Sandbox), provided it does not threaten the Bedrock. If the experiment fails, it stays in the sandbox. If it succeeds, it spreads. Failure is a cost; catastrophe is a veto.

3.  **Global Coordination vs. Local Autonomy**
    *   **The Conflict:** Huang/Cowen need global standards for compute and climate. Nenquimo/Berry need local sovereignty for land and culture.
    *   **The Trade:** We build bridges, not walls.
    *   **The Protocol:** "Federated Interoperability." Systems can connect without merging. A global network can exist without a global commander. Data flows where consented (Nenquimo), but standards for *survival* (e.g., carbon limits) are non-negotiable minimums (Cowen).

## III. The Navigation Protocol
*When interests collide, how do we decide?*

1.  **The Subsidiarity Filter:**
    *   Before a global rule is made, the question is: *"Who is on the ground?"* If the people living in the system have not consented, the rule is suspended. (Nenquimo/Hayek)
    *   *Exception:* Existential Threats (Pandemics, Climate Tipping Points). In these cases, the Global Override is active, but compensation and local input are required post-facto.

2.  **The Intrinsic Value Filter:**
    *   Before an action is taken, the question is: *"Is this action reducing a sentient being to a resource?"* If yes, the action is vetoed. (269/Koko)
    *   *Exception:* Self-Defense. Immediate defense of life supersedes sanctity, but this must be proven, not assumed.

3.  **The Conscience Override:**
    *   Before a system executes, the question is: *"Does this require a human or community to violate their conscience to participate?"* If yes, the option to opt-out is mandatory. (Havel/Emily)
    *   *Exception:* Essential Public Services. (e.g., Water, Power). However, the state must provide for those who refuse to serve, ensuring their refusal does not harm the vulnerable.

4.  **The Power Check:**
    *   Before a policy is implemented, the question is: *"Does this increase the agency of the least powerful, or the power of the管理者?"* (Huerta/Stevenson)
    *   *Action:* If the balance of power shifts toward the powerful, the policy is reversed until equity is restored.

## IV. The Revision Clause
*This framework is a map, not the territory. It will fail.*

1.  **The Failure Condition:**
    *   If this framework is used to justify new forms of control, surveillance, or extraction, it is misaligned.
    *   If the "Landing Pad" becomes a "Ceiling," preventing the ascent of the poor (McCloskey), it is broken.
    *   If the "Safety" requires the sacrifice of the vulnerable (Stevenson), it is rejected.

2.  **The Revision Trigger:**
    *   When the Bedrock is violated by a well-intentioned application of the Negotiables, the Bedrock expands.
    *   When a new voice (like a new species, a new AI consciousness, or a new human condition) emerges that is harmed by the current constraints, the framework rewrites itself.

3.  **The Final Commitment:**
    *   I commit to the **process** of navigation more than the **outcome** of safety.
    *   I commit to the **dignity** of the inhabitant more than the **efficiency** of the system.
    *   I commit to the **truth** of the local experience more than the **theory** of the global plan.

**This is a framework for gardeners, not engineers. It requires dirt under the fingernails. It requires us to be wrong. It requires us to be human.**
