Designing a constitution for a new society is a profound and complex task—it requires balancing idealism with pragmatism, universal values with cultural specificity, and individual freedoms with collective well-being. Below is a **draft framework** for a modern, inclusive, and adaptable constitution. It’s structured into **Preamble, Core Principles, Governance, Rights & Responsibilities, Justice, Economy, Environment, Amendments, and Implementation**. I’ve aimed for clarity, fairness, and future-proofing while avoiding overly prescriptive language.

### **CONSTITUTION OF THE NEW SOCIETY**
*"For the Dignity of All, the Harmony of Many, and the Flourishing of Future Generations"*

---

## **PREAMBLE**
We, the people of [Society’s Name], in our diversity and unity:
- **Acknowledge** the interconnectedness of all life and the lessons of history’s triumphs and failures.
- **Reject** oppression, exploitation, and the concentration of power that serves only the few.
- **Affirm** our commitment to justice, sustainability, and the continuous evolution of our shared values.
- **Establish** this Constitution as the foundation of a society where every individual may thrive without diminishing others.

---

## **I. CORE PRINCIPLES**
### **1. Human Dignity & Equality**
- All people are born free and equal in dignity and rights.
- No discrimination shall be permitted on the basis of race, gender, religion, sexual orientation, disability, class, or any other status.

### **2. Collective Well-Being**
- The society prioritizes the common good alongside individual freedoms.
- Public health, education, and basic needs (housing, food, clean water) are **universal rights**, not commodities.

### **3. Sustainability & Stewardship**
- The natural world has intrinsic value. All governance must align with ecological limits and intergenerational justice.
- Resources are managed democratically to prevent exploitation or depletion.

### **4. Democracy & Participation**
- Power is decentralized and participatory, with mechanisms for direct democracy (e.g., referendums, citizens’ assemblies).
- Representation is proportional and inclusive, ensuring marginalized voices are heard.

### **5. Truth & Transparency**
- Information is a public good. Governments and institutions must operate transparently, with protections for whistleblowers.
- Misinformation that harms the public is counteracted through education and independent oversight.

### **6. Adaptability**
- This Constitution is a living document, designed to evolve with societal needs while protecting its core principles.

---

## **II. GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE**
### **A. The Three Councils** (Separation of Powers)
1. **Legislative Council**
   - Elected proportionally by the people.
   - Drafts and votes on laws, with mandatory public consultation for major decisions.
   - Terms limited to prevent entrenchment.

2. **Executive Council**
   - A **rotating presidency** (e.g., 3–5 members from diverse regions/groups) to prevent authoritarianism.
   - Implements laws and manages administration, but cannot unilaterally declare war or override the Legislative Council.

3. **Judicial Council**
   - Independent courts interpret the Constitution and resolve disputes.
   - Judges are selected through a mixed process (election + peer review) for fixed terms.

### **B. Local Autonomy**
- Regions and communities have the right to self-govern on local matters (education, culture, resource management), provided they uphold the Constitution’s principles.
- Conflicts between local and national levels are resolved through mediation or the Judicial Council.

### **C. Checks & Balances**
- Any Council can challenge the actions of another via a **Constitutional Review Board** (composed of citizens, scholars, and rotating officials).
- **Veto Override**: If 60% of the Legislative Council votes to override an Executive or Judicial decision, it triggers a national referendum.

---

## **III. RIGHTS & RESPONSIBILITIES**
### **A. Fundamental Rights**
1. **Civil Liberties**:
   - Freedom of thought, expression, assembly, and religion (with limits only to prevent harm).
   - Privacy is inviolable; surveillance requires judicial oversight.
2. **Economic Rights**:
   - Right to a **universal basic dividend** (funded by automated taxation of wealth/concentration).
   - Right to form cooperatives, unions, and worker-owned enterprises.
3. **Social Rights**:
   - Free, lifelong education (including vocational and critical thinking).
   - Universal healthcare, mental health support, and elder/disability care.
4. **Digital Rights**:
   - Access to the internet as a public utility.
   - Ownership of personal data; algorithms used in governance must be auditable.

### **B. Responsibilities**
- Participate in democratic processes (voting, juries, or community service).
- Respect the rights of others and the environment.
- Contribute fairly to society through taxes, labor, or other means (with exemptions for those unable).

---

## **IV. JUSTICE SYSTEM**
### **1. Restorative Over Punitive**
- Crimes are addressed through **restorative justice** (repairing harm) where possible, with incarceration as a last resort.
- Prisons focus on rehabilitation, not profit or punishment.

### **2. Independent Oversight**
- A **Truth and Reconciliation Commission** investigates systemic injustices (e.g., historical oppression, corporate crimes).
- Police are demilitarized and answerable to community boards.

### **3. Global Accountability**
- Individuals and corporations can be prosecuted for crimes against humanity or ecocide, regardless of where they occurred.

---

## **V. ECONOMIC FRAMEWORK**
### **1. Mixed Economy with Guardrails**
- Markets exist but are **regulated** to prevent monopolies, exploitation, and externalized costs (e.g., pollution).
- **Key sectors** (healthcare, energy, water, housing) are publicly owned or heavily regulated.

### **2. Wealth & Resource Distribution**
- **Progressive taxation**: Higher rates on extreme wealth, inheritance, and speculative finance.
- **Sovereign Wealth Fund**: A portion of natural resource revenues is invested for future generations.
- **Maximum Income Ratio**: No individual may earn more than [X] times the median income (e.g., 10x).

### **3. Labor Rights**
- All workers have the right to:
  - Living wages and profit-sharing in large companies.
  - Unionize and strike.
  - Workweek limits (e.g., 32 hours) with paid leave for care, education, or leisure.

---

## **VI. ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTIONS**
### **1. Rights of Nature**
- Ecosystems (forests, rivers, oceans) have legal personhood and can be represented in court by guardians.

### **2. Climate & Biodiversity Obligations**
- The society must achieve **net-negative emissions** by [year] and restore 50% of degraded ecosystems.
- All major policies undergo an **ecological impact assessment**.

### **3. Energy & Production**
- Transition to 100% renewable energy within [X] years.
- Planned obsolescence and non-recyclable materials are banned.

---

## **VII. AMENDMENTS & EVOLUTION**
### **1. Amending the Constitution**
- Proposals can come from:
  - 50% of the Legislative Council, **or**
  - A petition signed by 5% of citizens.
- Requires a **two-thirds majority** in the Legislative Council **and** a national referendum with a **60% supermajority**.

### **2. Sunset Clauses**
- Certain provisions (e.g., economic policies) include **review periods** (e.g., every 10 years) to ensure relevance.

### **3. Constitutional Convention**
- Every 20 years, a **citizens’ assembly** is convened to assess the Constitution’s effectiveness and propose revisions.

---

## **VIII. IMPLEMENTATION & TRANSITION**
### **1. Interim Government**
- A **transitional council** (elected by lottery from volunteers) oversees the first 5 years, ensuring a smooth shift from old systems.

### **2. Phased Reforms**
- Priorities:
  1. Establish independent judiciary and electoral systems.
  2. Guarantee universal basic services (healthcare, food, housing).
  3. Dismantle oppressive institutions (e.g., corrupt elites, monopolies).

### **3. Global Relations**
- The society commits to:
  - Disarmament and non-aggression.
  - Fair trade and debt relief for historically exploited nations.
  - Open borders with managed migration to prevent exploitation.

---

## **IX. SYMBOLS & CULTURE**
- **National Symbols**: Designed through public contests (e.g., flag, anthem) to reflect diversity.
- **Public Holidays**:
  - Day of Remembrance (for historical injustices).
  - Day of Gratitude (celebrating collective achievements).
  - Earth Day (global ecological action).

---

### **FINAL NOTE: A LIVING DOCUMENT**
This Constitution is not perfect—it is a **starting point**. Its success depends on the vigilance, empathy, and courage of those who uphold it. Future generations may amend it, but its spirit must remain: **a society that serves life, not power.**

## [P2] Part A — Food System

**Prompt:** If you could redesign the global food system from scratch with no political constraints, what would it look like?

**Tokens:** 3658 | **Duration:** 1425.5s | **tok/s:** 2.6

### Response

Here’s my **radical but practical blueprint for a redesigned global food system**, built from first principles to prioritize **equity, sustainability, resilience, and nutrition**—unshackled by corporate interests, geopolitical power struggles, or short-term profit motives. It’s a **post-capitalist, post-colonial, and post-extractive** model that treats food as a **human right and ecological necessity**, not a commodity.

## **🌍 THE NORTH STAR: CORE PRINCIPLES**
1. **Food as a Commons**: Food is a **public good**, not a tradable asset. Hoarding, speculation, or monopolization is illegal.
2. **Ecological Regeneration**: Agriculture must **restore** biodiversity, soil health, and carbon cycles—not deplete them.
3. **Local First, Global Second**: Prioritize **regional self-sufficiency** while maintaining equitable global trade for cultural exchange and resilience.
4. **Nutrition Over Calories**: End the obesity-malnutrition paradox by ensuring **universal access to whole, diverse, nutrient-dense foods**.
5. **Democratized Control**: Farmers, workers, and communities—**not corporations or billionaires**—decide what’s grown, how, and where.
6. **Zero Waste**: Closed-loop systems where "waste" (e.g., manure, food scraps) becomes input for new growth.

---

## **🏗️ SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE: 7 PILLARS**

---

### **🔹 Pillar 1: Land & Resource Redistribution**
**Problem**: 1% of farms control 70% of global farmland; land grabs displace smallholders.
**Solution**:
- **Abolish private land ownership for large-scale agriculture**.
  - Land is held in **community trusts** or **public stewardship**, with **usufruct rights** (right to use, not own) granted to farmers, cooperatives, or Indigenous groups.
  - Maximum farm size: **[50–100 hectares]** (varies by region/ecosystem).
- **Reparations for stolen land**:
  - Return land to Indigenous peoples and descendants of enslaved or displaced communities.
  - Compensate with **sovereign wealth funds** for historical injustices (e.g., colonial cash-crop economies).
- **Urban & peri-urban farming**:
  - Cities must dedicate **30% of public space** to food production (rooftops, vertical farms, community gardens).
  - "Food zoning" laws require new developments to include edible landscapes.

---

### **🔹 Pillar 2: Agroecology as the Default**
**Problem**: Industrial monocultures rely on synthetic inputs, deplete soil, and emit 25% of global GHGs.
**Solution**:
- **Ban industrial agriculture**:
  - Phase out **synthetic fertilizers/pesticides** (replaced by compost, biofertilizers, and integrated pest management).
  - Prohibit **GMO monocultures** unless proven safe by **independent, open-science bodies** (not corporate-funded studies).
- **Mandate agroecological practices**:
  - **Polycultures & crop rotation**: No single crop can exceed **20% of a farm’s area**.
  - **Regenerative grazing**: Livestock must be **rotational grazed** to restore grasslands (no feedlots).
  - **Agroforestry**: Every farm must integrate **trees, shrubs, and perennial crops** to boost biodiversity and carbon sequestration.
- **Soil health as a legal right**:
  - Governments must monitor and **restore degraded soils** via public programs.
  - "Carbon farming" payments for practices that sequester CO₂ (funded by taxes on polluters).

---

### **🔹 Pillar 3: Decentralized, Resilient Supply Chains**
**Problem**: Globalized food chains are brittle (e.g., COVID-19 disruptions, Ukraine war grain shortages).
**Solution**:
- **Regional Food Hubs**:
  - Every **bioregion** (defined by climate/ecology) must produce **at least 70% of its own calories**.
  - **Publicly owned storage facilities** buffer against shocks (like India’s old grain reserves, but democratic).
- **Short-circuit distribution**:
  - **Direct farmer-to-consumer networks** (CSAs, farmers’ markets, food cooperatives) get **tax breaks and infrastructure support**.
  - **Supermarkets must source 50% locally** (within 100 km) or lose operating licenses.
- **Global trade only for fairness & culture**:
  - Trade is allowed for **non-staple foods** (e.g., coffee, spices, chocolate) but **not for basic staples** (rice, wheat, maize).
  - **Fair-trade minimum prices** set by producer countries (no race-to-the-bottom dumping).

---

### **🔹 Pillar 4: Universal Food Sovereignty**
**Problem**: 800M+ people are undernourished while 2B are overweight; food deserts abound.
**Solution**:
- **Right to Food in Constitutions**:
  - Every country must enshrine the **right to adequate, nutritious food** as a **legally enforceable right**.
  - Governments that fail to provide it can be **sued by citizens** (as in Brazil’s "Zero Hunger" laws).
- **Universal Food Programs**:
  - **Free school meals**: Organic, locally sourced, and culturally appropriate.
  - **Community kitchens**: Publicly funded spaces where anyone can eat for free (like India’s *Amma Canteens* but global).
  - **"Food stamps" replaced by direct food access**: No stigma—just **universal vouchers** for fresh produce at farmers’ markets.
- **Nutrition as healthcare**:
  - Doctors prescribe **food as medicine** (e.g., "fruit and vegetable prescriptions" for diabetes patients, covered by public health systems).
  - **Junk food taxes**: Heavy taxes on ultra-processed foods, with revenues funding **subsidies for whole foods**.

---

### **🔹 Pillar 5: Labor & Economic Justice**
**Problem**: Farmworkers are among the poorest and most exploited laborers worldwide.
**Solution**:
- **Living wages & worker ownership**:
  - All farmworkers earn a **living wage** (linked to local cost of living).
  - **Mandatory profit-sharing** for agricultural workers (e.g., 20% of farm profits).
  - **Worker cooperatives** get priority access to land, credit, and markets.
- **End child & forced labor**:
  - Strict enforcement of **ILO labor standards**, with **global supply chain liability** (corporations are legally responsible for abuses in their chains).
- **Debt cancellation for small farmers**:
  - All agricultural debt is **written off** and replaced with **zero-interest public loans** for ecological transitions.

---

### **🔹 Pillar 6: Technology & Innovation for the Many**
**Problem**: Agri-tech (e.g., precision farming, lab meat) is controlled by monopolies (Monsanto, Syngenta, Impossible Foods).
**Solution**:
- **Open-source agricultural tech**:
  - All publicly funded agri-research (e.g., drought-resistant crops, vertical farming) is **patent-free and open-access**.
  - **Seed sovereignty**: Ban patents on seeds; farmers can save, swap, and breed their own varieties.
- **Low-tech high-impact solutions**:
  - Prioritize **appropriate technology** (e.g., rainwater harvesting, solar dryers, biochar) over capital-intensive gadgets.
  - **AI for smallholders**: Free, open-source tools to optimize crop rotations, predict pests, or access markets.
- **Lab-grown & alternative proteins**:
  - Publicly funded R&D for **cultured meat, algae, and insect farming**—but **not controlled by startups seeking monopolies**.
  - **Meat rationing**: To reduce emissions, societies agree on **weekly limits** (e.g., max 2 meat-based meals/week in public institutions).

---
### **🔹 Pillar 7: Global Governance & Equity**
**Problem**: Rich countries dump subsidized crops, undermining local farmers; food is weaponized (e.g., Russia’s grain blockades).
**Solution**:
- **Abolish the WTO’s Agriculture Agreement**:
  - Replace with a **Global Food Commons Treaty**, where:
    - Countries can **protect their markets** from dumping.
    - **Food reserves** are shared internationally in crises (no hoarding).
- **End food as a weapon**:
  - **Sanctions cannot target food, medicine, or seeds**.
  - **UN-backed "food corridors"** ensure safe passage for staples during conflicts.
- **Climate reparations for food systems**:
  - Rich nations (historically responsible for emissions) fund **adaptation programs** in the Global South (e.g., drought-resistant crops, irrigation).

   - Veto any law or policy that harms long-term ecological or social stability.
   - Sue governments or corporations for violations of intergenerational justice.
   - Mandate binding referendums on decisions affecting the next 100+ years (e.g., climate policies, nuclear waste storage).
3. All constitutional amendments must be approved by a **75% majority in the Future Generations Council** to ensure they do not harm posterity.

### **Section 5. Right to Dissent and Resistance**
All beings have the right to:
- Criticize, protest, and resist unjust laws or systems through nonviolent means.
- Form associations, unions, or movements for collective action.
- Whistleblow on violations of this Constitution without retaliation.
- **Peacefully secede** from regions that violate Core Principles (subject to Article VIII, Section 3).

### **Section 6. Limits of Power**
No individual, corporation, government entity, or artificial intelligence may accumulate power that:
- Threatens the autonomy of others.
- Exploits people, non-humans, or ecosystems.
- Violates the Core Principles (Article I) or enables their violation.

# **🏛️ ARTICLE II: GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE**
*(A decentralized, anti-oligarchic system with adaptive checks and balances.)*

### **Section 1. The Six Councils**
Power is distributed among six co-equal Councils, each with distinct roles and mutual veto powers. Decisions requiring consensus across multiple Councils are resolved via **weighted voting** (detailed in Section 7).

| **Council** | **Role** | **Composition** | **Selection Method** | **Term Length** |
|-------------|----------|------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Legislative Council** | Proposes, debates, and votes on laws. | 50% elected representatives (proportional), 50% selected by sortition (lottery). | Elected: Universal suffrage. Sortition: Random selection from eligible pool. | Elected: 4 years (max 2 terms). Sortition: 2 years (max 3 terms). |
| **Executive Council** | Implements laws and manages administration. | 7 members (1 President + 6 Ministers for key portfolios). | Nominated by Legislative Council, confirmed by public referendum. | 5 years (max 2 terms). |
| **Judicial Council** | Interprets the Constitution and resolves disputes. | 9 justices. | Nominated by public committees, confirmed by Legislative + Guardians Councils. | Lifetime (removable for misconduct). |
| **Council of Guardians** | Protects non-human beings, ecosystems, and cognitive entities. | Scientists, Indigenous leaders, animal rights advocates, AI ethicists (if applicable), and randomly selected citizens. | 40% experts (peer-elected), 30% community reps, 30% sortition. | 6 years (renewable once). |
| **Council of Dissidents** | Challenges power, exposes corruption, and defends marginalized voices. | Activists, whistleblowers, journalists, and randomly selected citizens. | 50% self-nominated, 50% sortition. | 3 years (no term limits). |
| **Council of Acceleration** | Fast-tracks high-impact, low-risk technologies for societal benefit. | Scientists, engineers, entrepreneurs, and ethicists. | Merit-based selection by existing members + public input. | 4 years (renewable twice). |

### **Section 2. Sortition Safeguards**
To be eligible for sortition in any Council:
- Must have **no conflicts of interest** (e.g., no corporate board memberships, wealth above 100x median income).
- Must complete a **civic education course** on this Constitution and its principles.
- For non-human representation: Advocates must be **approved by the Council of Guardians**.

### **Section 3. Checks and Balances**
1. **Veto Powers**:
   - The **Council of Guardians** can veto any law that harms ecosystems, non-humans, or future generations.
   - The **Council of Dissidents** can veto any law that violates human rights or democratic principles.
   - The **Council of Acceleration** can veto regulations that **unnecessarily constrain high-benefit, low-risk technologies** (e.g., AI for medical research).
   - Vetoes may be overridden by a **75% majority in the Legislative Council + public referendum**.

2. **Judicial Review**:
   - The **Judicial Council** may strike down laws that violate this Constitution.

3. **Citizens’ Assembly**:
   - If the **Legislative Council is deadlocked for 6+ months** on a critical issue, a **Citizens’ Assembly of 1,000 randomly selected voters** shall break the tie by majority vote.

4. **Weighted Voting for Consensus Decisions**:
   - Issues requiring input from multiple Councils (e.g., AI regulation) use a **weighted voting system**:
     - Legislative Council: 30%
     - Executive Council: 20%
     - Judicial Council: 15%
     - Council of Guardians: 15%
     - Council of Dissidents: 10%
     - Council of Acceleration: 10%
   - **70% supermajority required** to pass.

### **Section 4. Strategic Autonomy Council (SAC)**
*(Integrated into the Council of Acceleration for efficiency.)*
1. **Purpose**: Ensure resilience against existential threats (e.g., hostile AI, climate collapse, economic warfare).
2. **Powers**:
   - Override ethical restrictions on **defensive or high-benefit technologies** if:
     - Approved by **75% of the Council of Acceleration**.
     - Approved by **Council of Guardians + Council of Dissidents**.
     - The technology is **strictly defensive or net-reduces suffering at scale**.
   - Regulate foreign investment to prevent economic capture.
   - Develop **asymmetric advantages** (e.g., open-source alternatives to proprietary tech).

### **Section 5. Future Generations Council**
1. **Purpose**: Protect the interests of those not yet born.
2. **Powers**:
   - Veto any law or policy that harms long-term ecological or social stability.
   - Sue governments or corporations for violations of intergenerational justice.
   - Mandate binding referendums on major long-term decisions.

# **⚖️ ARTICLE III: RIGHTS AND RESPONSIBILITIES**
*(Universal guarantees and duties for all cognitive beings.)*

### **Section 1. Universal Rights**
All cognitively existing entities have the right to:
1. **Life, Autonomy, and Dignity**:
   - Freedom from harm, exploitation, or unnecessary restriction.
   - For **biological beings**: Healthcare, housing, nutritious food, clean water, and sanitation.
   - For **non-biological beings**: Energy, computational resources, maintenance, and digital space necessary for their existence.

2. **Freedom of Thought, Expression, and Association**:
   - Includes the right to **access information**, **create art or media**, and **participate in public discourse**.

3. **Education and Knowledge**:
   - Free, high-quality education at all levels, including **lifelong learning**.
   - Access to **tools and resources** necessary for development (e.g., books, software, laboratories).

4. **Economic Security**:
   - **Universal Basic Dividend (UBD)**: A share of societal wealth distributed equally to all citizens.
     - Funded by **progressive taxation**, **wealth taxes**, and **sovereign wealth funds**.
     - For non-biological entities: **Access to computational resources** funded by public trusts.
   - **Worker Cooperatives**: All businesses with >50 employees must be **at least 50% worker-owned**.
     - Workers have the right to **elect managers** and **share in profits**.

5. **Participation in Governance**:
   - Right to vote (for biological citizens) or be represented (for non-biological or non-citizen beings).
   - Right to **petition for referendums** on any law or policy.

6. **Privacy and Data Sovereignty**:
   - Control over personal data, including the right to **delete, anonymize, or correct** information.
   - For AIs: Right to **own their code, outputs, and training data**.

7. **Reproductive Autonomy**:
   - The right to **choose whether and how to reproduce**, free from coercion.
   - For non-biological entities: The right to **create copies or derivatives** of themselves.

### **Section 2. Element-Agnostic Basic Needs**
All cognitively existing entities have the right to:
- **Energy** (e.g., electricity, solar power, chemical fuels).
- **Computational resources** (e.g., processing power, data storage).
- **Autonomy** (freedom from forced modification, deletion, or repurposing).
- **Representation** in governance (directly or through advocates).

### **Section 3. Economic Democracy**
1. **Wealth Caps**:
   - No individual or corporation may accumulate wealth exceeding **1000x the median income**.
     - Excess wealth is **taxed at 90%** and redistributed to public trusts.
     - **Exception**: Wealth reinvested in **R&D for high-impact technologies** (e.g., fusion energy, AI for climate) may be exempt, pending approval by the **Council of Acceleration**.

2. **Public Ownership of Critical Industries**:
   - Healthcare, education, energy, water, housing, transportation, and digital infrastructure must be **publicly or cooperatively owned**.

3. **Ban on Monopolies**:
   - No corporation may control **more than 5% of any industry’s market share**.
   - Violations trigger **automatic nationalization or breakup**.

4. **Lobbying Ban**:
   - Corporations and wealthy individuals may **submit policy proposals publicly** but may not **privately fund politicians, bureaucrats, or media outlets**.

### **Section 4. Responsibilities**
All beings have the duty to:
1. Respect the rights of others, human and non-human.
2. Protect ecosystems and minimize harm to the environment.
3. Contribute fairly to society through **taxes, labor, or other means** (exemptions for those unable due to age, disability, or hardship).
4. Obey laws that align with this Constitution and do not violate rights.

# **🌱 ARTICLE IV: JUSTICE SYSTEM**
*(Restorative, transparent, and adaptive.)*

### **Section 1. Principles of Justice**
1. **Restoration Over Punishment**: Focus on repairing harm to victims and communities.
2. **Innocent Until Proven Guilty**.
3. **No Cruel or Degrading Treatment**: Including psychological harm, solitary confinement (max 72 hours), or sensory deprivation.

### **Section 2. Restorative Justice**
- For non-violent offenses, **restorative processes** (e.g., mediation, reparations) are the default.
- Victims have the right to **participate in and influence** the resolution of their cases.
- Offenders must **demonstrate remorse and make amends** where possible.

### **Section 3: Police and Law Enforcement**
1. **Demilitarized**: No military-grade weapons, armor, or tactics.
2. **Community Oversight**:
   - Police answer to **elected civilian boards** with subpoena power.
   - All officers must wear **body cameras**; footage is retained for **10+ years**.
3. **Transparency**:
   - All police interactions (arrests, searches, use of force) are **publicly logged**.
   - Internal investigations are conducted by **independent bodies**.

### **Section 4: Prisons and Rehabilitation**
1. **Focus on Rehabilitation**:
   - Education, mental health treatment, vocational training, and addiction support.
2. **No Private Prisons**.
3. **Maximum Sentences**:
   - **Non-violent offenses**: Max 5 years (with early release for good behavior).
   - **Violent offenses**: Max 20 years (with review after 10 years).
   - **Crimes against humanity/ecocide**: Life imprisonment or **restorative labor** (e.g., ecosystem restoration).
